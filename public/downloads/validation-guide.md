## Review method

1. Freeze the exact course/source version, case packet and reference dates. Record the files or app views inspected.
2. Inspect every module's objective, prerequisite, taught calculation, case, rubric and transfer prompt. Record coverage at module level; do not extrapolate a sample inspection into approval of all 26 modules.
3. Independently reconstruct the flagship's cash balances, scenario table, severe boundary and revised feasibility decisions. Check all new pilot form tables before administering them.
4. Inspect contract mechanics, lifecycle and funding in detail. Then inspect model scope, execution assumptions and research interpretation with appropriate specialists where needed.
5. Assess representative learner work using the four-dimension rubric below. Preserve quotations and calculation checks supporting each score.
6. Resolve critical findings, rerun the affected exercises, and record the reviewer’s actual final decision. Unresolved items remain visible.

Software verification evidence can inform the review, but the reviewer should inspect its coverage and limitations. A passing test is not proof that the explanation teaches well.

## Analytic rubric for written case work

Use **0–3 per dimension**, matching the current app review desk. A missing score is blank, not zero. Record an exact quotation or a reproducible calculation as evidence. A sum out of 12 is descriptive; unresolved contract or funding errors cannot be compensated for by polished prose.

| Dimension | 0: missing or materially wrong | 1: partial or insufficiently defended | 2: supported with stated limitations | 3: reproducible, challenged and defended |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Contract, units and calculation | Material unit, obligation, position or cash result is wrong or absent. | Some working is correct, but missing steps or an unresolved error prevent reproduction. | Correct contract terms and principal cash/scenario calculations are shown, with relevant assumptions and limits. | A second reader can reproduce the complete ledger; boundary/adverse cases and changed-input checks support the result. |
| Objective and alternatives | No usable mandate or fair comparison; a strategy label substitutes for a decision. | An objective and alternative are named, but the comparison omits a material tradeoff or changes resources. | A recommendation follows the stated objective and compares a relevant alternative under common resources. | The strongest opposing alternative is fairly represented, and the conditions favoring either choice are defended. |
| Risk and evidence | Unsupported certainty, concealed assumptions, or missing funding/operational exposure changes the conclusion. | Risk categories are named without showing their consequence, or evidence scope is overstated. | Known facts, assumptions, costs, uncertainty and relevant operational limits are distinguished. | A consequential adverse scenario and invalidation condition are tested; source/evidence limits constrain the conclusion. |
| Revision and communication | The first answer is missing or the update is ignored or silently substituted. | A revision is asserted with weak causal explanation or an unclear audit trail. | Initial and revised views are preserved and the update produces an explained revision or defended continuation. | A concise defense addresses a serious challenge, reproduces its numerical effect and identifies any remaining uncertainty. |

**Critical flags:** wrong deliverable or exercise cash; unexplained missing position; invented fill; failure to meet a stated cash deadline; use of future information; unreported tried variants; an unsupported guarantee. Identify the exact flag and affected conclusion. Require remediation and a changed follow-up case. The reviewer may still score other dimensions, while leaving acceptance pending.

# Advanced Options Academy: teaching and assessment standard

This standard develops the existing 26-module advanced curriculum into an MBA-style finance specialization. It describes teaching quality and evidence of learning; it does not claim accreditation, independent expert sign-off or demonstrated learning gains before those reviews take place.

The canonical teaching packages are in `course-app/content/graduate.json`. Each preserves the relevant lesson from the four teaching volumes and adds a decision bridge, an original fictional case, staged information, a reproducible debrief, submission examples and reviewer questions. Module 10, **The Meridian Investment Committee**, is the flagship integrated seminar. Its alternatives use the same starting wealth, stated quote sides, premiums, fees and cash constraints.

## The teaching cycle

1. **Orient:** explain the decision the concept helps answer; name prerequisites and an observable learning outcome.
2. **Diagnose:** record the learner's intuition before showing a solution.
3. **Teach:** build an intuitive explanation, reproduce a worked calculation and identify a common misconception.
4. **Practice:** use guided and independent exercises with explicit units, assumptions and permitted conclusions.
5. **Decide:** submit a recommendation using only the initial packet, including alternatives and missing evidence.
6. **Adapt:** reveal the update, preserve the first memo and explain a revised decision or defended continuation.
7. **Defend:** answer a skeptical reviewer's numerical, operational and interpretive challenges.
8. **Transfer:** solve an unfamiliar case later, noting assistance and any previously seen solution.

The beginner route remains an accessible entry point. The advanced reference pace of four weeks per module is a planning aid. A completed reading, a saved submission, a correct arithmetic result and an independently defended project are different evidence states.

## Case and calculation requirements

Every original case must label fictional data, identify the relevant contract and accounting assumptions, and expose missing information rather than fill it with favorable guesses. A quote, a model value, an order intention, a confirmed fill and an expiration payoff must remain distinct. Portfolio comparisons include retained cash and use a common starting-resource basis. Gross funding and maximum modeled loss are different quantities.

Before release, an author or reviewer reconstructs each new numerical case independently. The flagship case requires a complete scenario table, severe boundary, fee reconciliation and updated cash-feasibility check. A change to a contract, quote side, fee convention or mandate requires rechecking all affected outputs.

## Feedback standard

Feedback identifies the misconception, explains its consequence and assigns a corrective task. A student who calculates correctly but omits the objective, alternatives, uncertainty or operational obligations has not completed the full analysis. Annotated weak, adequate and excellent samples make that distinction visible. Samples are teaching aids; copying them is not independent evidence.

The case rubric has four dimensions: contracts/units/calculation; objective/alternatives; risk/evidence; and revision/communication. Score each **0 absent or materially wrong, 1 partial with unresolved errors, 2 supported with stated limitations, 3 independently reproducible, challenged and clearly defended**. Record specific evidence for each score. Arithmetic safety errors require correction and a fresh case; a high total does not compensate for unresolved funding or contract mistakes.

Automated practice can grade narrowly specified numerical questions. It cannot certify the quality of an open-ended investment recommendation. Self-reflection is labeled self-review, and a human reviewer must actually inspect the work before it is described as independently reviewed. The app's saved submissions and practice attempts support this distinction.

## Capstone and committee defense

The capstone preserves the original question, A/B/C evidence level, methods, inputs, versions, trial log, assumptions, calculations, exceptions, failed alternatives and revisions. A reviewer should be able to change one input and ask the learner to explain the effect. A negative or inconclusive research result can meet the standard when its conclusion follows from complete, reproducible evidence.

Synthetic tests demonstrate stipulated software/accounting behavior. Published-series work supports a methodology-aware comparison. Historical-contract conclusions need suitable point-in-time contract evidence and qualified execution assumptions. None of these labels establishes future returns.

## Release and teaching evidence

Software verification checks functionality, isolation, persistence and calculations. Learner validation checks whether the instruction improves unfamiliar-case performance and retention. These are separate claims. Follow the learner pilot protocol (included course reference: GRADUATE_PILOT_PROTOCOL.md) before describing the course as proven effective.

Teaching benchmarks: [HBS case method](https://www.hbs.edu/mba/academic-experience/the-case-method) for preparation, discussion, decisions and reflection; [MIT Finance Theory I syllabus](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/15-401-finance-theory-i-fall-2008/pages/syllabus/) for analytical finance content and graded work. These references are design benchmarks, not affiliations or endorsements.
