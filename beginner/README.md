# Options, one small lesson at a time

**Start with [Lesson 01: your first goal is understanding](01_MARKETS.md).** You need no trading experience, brokerage account, coding, or real money. A notebook and ordinary calculator are enough.

This course teaches **U.S. stock and ETF options** from zero in **64 small lessons across 8 modules**. The default is **10–15 minutes per study day, five days a week**. Read one lesson, work its example, try the new question, and explain the idea in your own words before opening the answer. Use the remaining minutes for recall. If the lesson takes longer, split it across sessions.

The [84-session review plan](REVIEW_PLAN.md) includes 64 lesson sessions, 8 module checkpoints, and 12 review sessions. At five sessions a week it occupies about **17 weeks before extra retries or split sessions**. This is a planning example, not a deadline or a promise of readiness. Repeat an idea until you can explain it. Completing this course measures learning; it does not establish a profitable strategy or approve live trading.

Every lesson has a worked example, a fresh question, and a folded explanation. Every module has a checkpoint with a separate folded key. Save your first answer before checking. Correct every funding, share, expiry, and order-status error; a correct guess without an explanation is a prompt to review.

## Keep the first day small

Open Lesson 01, read the example, answer its question, and write one sentence explaining what you learned. Stop there if your study time is up. You do not need to open the rest of this library today.

## Scope and expectations

Prices, symbols, probabilities, fills, and accounts in exercises are invented unless explicitly identified as real dated observations. Most arithmetic starts with $0 teaching fees so you can learn one idea; real trading must include costs and exact contract terms. Advanced math and Python are optional extensions. You can finish with a well-reasoned decision to continue observing or not trade options.

Mechanics are checked against the SEC, OCC/OIC, FINRA, and exchange specifications. Read the [verification report](../VERIFICATION_REPORT.md) for what was checked and what remains account-specific. The [OCC disclosure document](https://www.theocc.com/company-information/documents-and-archives/options-disclosure-document) explains the official characteristics and risks and must be read before actual options trading.
