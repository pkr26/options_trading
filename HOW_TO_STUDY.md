# Study options in small daily sessions

Start with [Lesson 01](beginner/01_MARKETS.md). Study one idea at a time for **10–15 minutes**. Each lesson has teaching, a worked example and a new problem with a folded answer. Read eight lessons, then take that module's checkpoint.

## Learn one idea and use it

1. Recall the previous lesson without notes for a minute or two.
2. Read today's lesson. Write down any word you cannot explain; consult the [glossary](beginner/GLOSSARY.md) only as needed.
3. Cover the worked result and redo it. Keep dollars, shares and contracts separate.
4. Answer the fresh question before opening its solution. Record your first answer even when uncertain.
5. Compare reasoning as well as the final number. Correct the exact mistake, then explain the idea in one sentence.

Stop when the session ends. If you need another day on the same concept, take it. Use the [workbook](beginner/WORKBOOK.md) for a lightweight paper record or save notes in the app. Your existing [learning record](MY_LEARNING_RECORD.md) remains available; the course does not fill in completion claims for you.

## Use checkpoints as feedback

The written modules include five-question checkpoints with folded solutions. The app has server-graded five-question checkpoints with two alternating forms. Complete all eight lessons before taking one. In the app, **5/5** passes; the next module's completion controls then open. You can preview any lesson while working toward a pass.

Write answers before checking feedback. After a miss, revisit the relevant lesson, do a changed-number problem and try the alternate form. After both forms have been seen, they are review practice; their results no longer count as unseen evidence. For a fresh test, ask a tutor to change numbers and conditions and withhold the key. A score measures this learning task, not profitability or live readiness.

The final capstone in Lesson 63 has several cases. Do one case per session if needed. Explain the account's remaining shares, options and cash, not just a profit number.

## Review before forgetting

The app first schedules a completed lesson for review after one day. Successful recalls extend the interval to three, seven, fourteen and then thirty days; a missed recall brings it back after one day. These intervals are course design choices. Reveal the answer only after trying to explain the idea without notes.

Use [the 84-session plan](beginner/REVIEW_PLAN.md) if you prefer a written schedule. At five sessions a week it is about seventeen weeks before repeats and extra capstone time. The number is a planning aid, not a promise of mastery by a date. Review queues can take priority over a new lesson.

## Ask for teaching in chat

Say **“Teach me Lesson 12 in five minutes. Explain one idea, show one example, ask a new question, and wait.”** After answering, ask for feedback on your reasoning. For a checkpoint, say **“Give me a fresh Module 3 quiz without showing the answers.”** Record any attempt you want to keep in your notes or workbook.

## Keep practice honest

Study fictional examples first. When later observing a real quote, record its source, time and delay. Paper decisions can be logged in a notebook; no broker account or deposit is needed. Keep rejected ideas and unfilled orders. Simulated fills and the [synthetic Python labs](labs/README.md) can teach a process but cannot establish executable market performance.

Once the beginner course is complete, select an [optional advanced path](MASTERY_TRACKS.md) only if it serves your goal. The original [advanced study guide](ADVANCED_STUDY_GUIDE.md) describes the longer research program. The beginner course does not require calculus or Python.

## Advanced case seminars in the app

Open **Advanced academy** for the 26-module teaching pathway. Begin with the prerequisite guidance for the selected module; Module 10 provides a complete Trade Design case seminar. Read its teaching sections, examine the exhibits, and save a first recommendation with calculations and risks before opening the new information. Save a revised submission after the update, then compare your reasoning with the rubric, annotated examples, and debrief. A defensible choice can differ from the model submission if its assumptions and calculations support it.

Use numeric practice to locate a specific misconception. Keep the first answer and feedback in your evidence history, revisit its teaching section, and solve a changed case. Come back at least seven days later for a retention check. The app distinguishes numeric practice from written work awaiting review. Bring major submissions and the capstone to a knowledgeable reviewer, using the defense prompts to challenge the reasoning.

Save drafts before leaving the academy. The advanced evidence export preserves submissions, revisions, numeric feedback, and the version of the teaching packet used. For offline study, download the advanced Markdown course from the academy; it is also included in the full course ZIP.
