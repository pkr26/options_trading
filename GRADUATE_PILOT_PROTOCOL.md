# Advanced course learner pilot protocol

**Status: protocol prepared; learner pilot not yet conducted.** No participants, reviewer approvals, learning gains or retention outcomes are invented. Technical release checks do not substitute for learner evidence.

The app's **Teaching studio → Learning pilot** implements the numerical component: a frozen three-module baseline, changed parallel numerical forms, server-enforced day-7/day-30 windows, real observed scores, consent, prior experience/exposure, self-reported confidence, assistance, minutes and clarity, missing measurements and a private export. Day 7 is available on elapsed days 7–13 and day 30 on elapsed days 30–44. This component is repeated practice on known item families. The written transfer cases, participant recruitment and independent reviewer calibration below remain activities for real people. Use the review desk and capstone artifacts to preserve their actual evidence.

## First question

Does the Trade Design teaching package help learners make and explain a coherent portfolio recommendation on a fresh case, including cash constraints and a response to new information? The study concerns learning, not investment performance.

## Participants and baseline

Recruit volunteers spanning relevant entry levels and record prior options, finance and programming experience. Record the recruited and completed counts and reasons for withdrawal where volunteered. Obtain participants' agreement to the pilot and state what records will be retained. Avoid collecting personal financial details; use supplied fictional portfolios.

Before instruction, administer an unfamiliar parallel case assessing objective interpretation, units, scenario accounting, alternatives, uncertainty and communication. Ask for confidence and assistance used. Save the first attempt before feedback. Do not use the flagship case's publicly visible debrief as an unseen baseline.

## Learning and assessment sequence

1. Complete the Trade Design package with session start/end times and interruptions recorded voluntarily.
2. Capture the initial memo, revised memo, practice attempts and assistance used.
3. Administer a fresh parallel transfer case after instruction. Keep its answer key withheld until submission.
4. Administer different parallel cases approximately 7 and 30 days later. Record actual elapsed days rather than claiming exact scheduling when a visit is late.
5. Ask learners which explanation, exhibit or interaction was confusing and which error they could now explain unaided.

A parallel form must test the same underlying skill with a material change in conditions, such as a different cash need, contract quantity, exercise assumption or objective. Changed numbers alone help arithmetic practice but may not test conceptual transfer. Review all form calculations independently before use.

## Measures and interpretation

Primary evidence is the change in independently rated fresh-case reasoning using the four-dimension teaching rubric. Report each dimension separately. Also report exact arithmetic/units accuracy, cash-ledger errors, assumption omissions, incomplete submissions, time spent, assistance and retention. Distinguish independent work from assisted or already-seen practice.

Report sample sizes, missing follow-ups and the distribution of results, not just a selected average. A small convenience pilot can identify usability and teaching problems; it does not establish broad effectiveness or isolate the cause of a change. If a larger comparison is later needed, preregister its question, assignment method, outcomes, exclusions and analysis before collecting results.

## Reviewer calibration

Have at least two knowledgeable reviewers independently score a shared subset without seeing the learner's earlier scores. Compare disagreements and preserve both initial scores. Clarify rubric language using specific examples before judging remaining work. Do not label self-scored answers as independent review. A numerical error that changes funding feasibility requires remediation even if prose is strong.

## Decisions after the pilot

Revise explanations, example order or difficulty when errors cluster around the same concept. Repair any incorrect case or calculator immediately and preserve the earlier version. Repeat the affected fresh checks after the correction. Set performance targets only after reviewing a baseline; do not retroactively select a flattering threshold.

The pilot report should identify changes made, evidence supporting them, unresolved weaknesses and the next evaluation question. Publish only aggregate findings that participants agreed to share. Course completion, engagement, paper profit and time spent are not substitutes for demonstrated learning.
