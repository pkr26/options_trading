# Options Academy course and application review

November 27, 2026

**The course is ready for serious self-study. I would fix two teaching defects and strengthen how the app measures independent work before using it to assess learners.** In one exercise, the tutor rejects an answer that follows from the only prices the learner has seen. At one supported input, the pricing lab understates gamma by 87.4%. Both survived the existing test suite.

The course's most distinctive asset is its decision cases. In the flagship seminar a learner allocates the same $110,000 across alternatives, commits a recommendation, then learns that the portfolio needs $8,000 in immediately accessible cash. A learner can make a defensible decision, discover why it no longer works, and explain the revision. I would make that experience available from the beginner course onward. ## Teaching defects to fix first

| Priority | Evidence and learner consequence | Acceptance check |
|---|---|---|
| **1. State all inputs in each issued question** | In advanced Module 11, the learner sees one fill at $2.00 and another at $2.20. After help on the cash question, the transfer prompt says fees are now $2 per filled contract. The visible figures give $424. The tutor silently uses new fills of $2.05 and $2.25 and grades $434 as correct. The ordinary three-question practice set presents the questions together. The error arises in the one-at-a-time tutor. [Fill questions](../../course-app/lib/graduate-practice.ts#L73) · [Transfer transition](../../course-app/lib/adaptive-teaching.ts#L123) | Store and render the actual contract, fills and fees alongside each issued item. An independent grader using only the visible prompt must reproduce its key on first attempt, retry, transfer and reload. |
| **2. Correct the European sensitivities** | At $1 spot, $1 strike, one calendar day, 1% annual volatility and zero rates and dividends, the lab gives gamma of 95.8236. The analytic value is 762.1781. A fixed one-cent move is large relative to the price interval over which delta changes here. At the same inputs with a permitted 30% rate, computed call delta is about 0.541 versus 0.942 analytically. The error affects the sensitivity shown before the learner even applies a shock. [Pricing lab](../../course-app/components/graduate-labs.tsx#L43) · [Finite differences](../../course-app/lib/graduate-models.ts#L24) | Use analytic European delta and gamma, or a method with verified convergence. Assert error bounds across the supported input range, including low-priced, near-expiry and low-volatility cases. Test local sensitivities separately from finite-shock repricing. |
| **3. Keep practice attached to its module** | With a delayed Module 10 practice response, navigating to Module 1 can display Module 10 questions under the Module 1 URL. Submission uses the returned practice set's module ID. A learner can do work for a different module from the one they selected. [Selection and request](../../course-app/components/graduate-course.tsx#L146) · [Submission](../../course-app/components/graduate-course.tsx#L190) | Reject obsolete responses or key them to their originating pane and module. Delay and reorder requests in a browser test and verify the heading, questions and saved record agree. |
| **4. Define portfolio risk units precisely** | Module 24 asks learners to add the share-equivalent deltas of positions A and B without identifying the underlying. The answer is meaningful for the same underlying. Across names, learners need a specified risk factor or per-name price shocks before combining dollar effects. The portfolio stress lab already teaches this distinction. [Graded question](../../course-app/lib/graduate-practice.ts#L143) · [Portfolio lab](../../course-app/components/portfolio-research-labs.tsx#L21) | Include one same-underlying question and one multi-underlying question. Require the learner to name the risk factor, supply units and recognize when an aggregate cannot be calculated from the supplied facts. |

Two smaller corrections would improve financial judgment. The strategy lab accepts a $200 covered-call premium on $100 stock and labels the resulting collar as fitting a zero budget. The ledger correctly follows those stipulated inputs, but the example also needs a quote-plausibility check. Module 7 can print gamma as `0.040100000000000004` in an issued question. Format stated input precision and grade using those same displayed values. ## How to build a leading course in this scope

**Make the product promise specific.** A graduate should be able to read a U.S. listed equity or ETF contract, select or decline a position under a stated mandate, reconcile the account through adverse events, and defend the limits of their evidence. Index options make a valuable contrast because product terms change the answer. Futures and international options can have separately scoped electives if demand warrants them. This preserves a coherent core for someone starting from zero.

OCC Learning already offers a structured sequence through contracts, strategies and Greeks. Cboe offers experience-based starting points, expert instruction and interactive tools. MIT's finance problem sets provide an academic comparison for the quantitative track. The opportunity here is to let one learner carry a decision all the way from contract selection to an independently reviewed account reconciliation, then repeat it on a genuinely different case. These comparisons use the publishers' public offerings. A defensible comparative ranking would require observed learner outcomes on common tasks. [OCC Learning](https://www.optionseducation.org/theoptionseducationcenter/occ-learning) · [Cboe Options Institute](https://www.cboe.com/optionsinstitute/) · [MIT finance assignments](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/15-450-analytics-of-finance-fall-2010/pages/assignments/)

### Give each learner a distinct capstone

| Learner | Signature assignment | Evidence that passes |
|---|---|---|
| Portfolio decision maker | Compare doing nothing, reducing stock, buying protection and writing a collar with the same starting resources. Release a changed cash demand or early-assignment event after the initial memo. | A reproducible comparison with fees and quote sides, a feasible cash plan, surviving positions, a defended choice and an explicit condition that changes it. |
| Independent researcher | Freeze a hypothesis before revealing a historical episode. Supply a dated, redistribution-cleared chain or use a learner-provided licensed sample. Require a pass/no-trade path, partial fills, costs and a genuinely later evaluation. | Another learner can reconstruct eligible decisions, rejected and unfilled orders, benchmark, sample separation and uncertainty. The learner can explain precisely which conclusions the data support. |
| Quantitative practitioner | Compare a European benchmark with an American-style lattice around a discrete dividend. Inspect convergence, sensitivity units and hedge P&L through an observed or explicitly synthetic event. | Independent reference prices, boundary checks, an error-versus-step-size table, a cash/share hedge ledger and an oral or written defense of model limitations. |

The written course already describes all three tracks. In the app, the 25 advanced modules after Module 1 each name their immediate predecessor as their prerequisite. Add a short placement case and a map of the skills each track actually needs. Credit prior evidence and expose the next gap. [Existing mastery paths](../../MASTERY_TRACKS.md) · [Advanced curriculum](../../course-app/content/graduate.json)

### Build two more cases at the depth of Trade Design

1. **Contract lifecycle and execution.** Start with a dated contract specification and a timestamped chain. Have the learner distinguish an ETF option from a cash-settled index series, then follow one position through an order, partial fill, changed liquidity, corporate action or assignment. At each event require a prediction, show the next event, and ask for cash, shares, outstanding options and funding deadline. Use the operational casebook and execution episodes as the starting material. [Operational cases](../../OPERATIONAL_CASEBOOK.md) · [Execution practice](../../EXECUTION_PRACTICE.md) · [Cboe series specifications](https://www.cboe.com/tradable_products/sp_500/spx_weekly_options/specifications/)
2. **Research from data intake to defense.** Start with a plausible result from a flawed file. The learner must find stale quotes, future information or repeated holdout use, repair the study, include unfilled decisions, and defend a smaller or inconclusive result. The current executable fixture provides one synthetic contract, 13 quote rows and six decisions. Keep it for hand checks and add one rights-cleared historical episode with a source manifest and dated field definitions. [Synthetic fixture](../../labs/data/manifest.json) · [Research standards](../../RESEARCH_LAB.md) · [Cboe data specifications](https://datashop.cboe.com/option-quote-intervals)

For each case, teach with a full worked example, then a partially completed ledger, then an unfamiliar independent case. Save the prediction and model inputs before reveal. Show why the learner's specific answer failed, and return a week later with a changed obligation. The app's four core advanced labs already ask for predictions, but retain them in component state. A permanent record would connect lab work to the review and capstone systems. [Prediction component](../../course-app/components/graduate-labs.tsx#L15) · [IES guidance on worked examples, retrieval and spacing](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/1)

**The first investment I would fund is Lesson 63 as an assessed, resumable account ledger, immediately after the correctness fixes.** The scenarios, worked solutions, scheduling machinery and evidence storage already exist. The result would show whether a learner can independently reconstruct the account after an unexpected event.
